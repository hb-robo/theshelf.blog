I've been thinking a bit about labor recently when it comes to projects that spawn a community. Increasingly, I read sentiments stating or implying the observation of such projects - or, in most cases, consumption - is either a form of labor or roughly as meritous.

--- 

Labor, as far as I understand it, exists in the gravitational pull of ambition. "Work" in the physics sense applies to the effort made against something in order to change something about it. Since we are not arbitrary creatures, and have some logic to our actions either personal or socialized, work is relative to concrete goals and defines progress towards them.

The way I see it, there are three barriers to something being done, and thus to begin an act of labor.

The first is the hardest to tread softly about: skill, or rather, ableness. Obviously, there is a prerequisite to any act of labor that the laborer be able to accomplish the task, either having that knowledge upfront or gaining it through effort on the job.

The second is time. Painting the walls of the Library of Babel may not be particularly ability-intensive (beyond physical ableness), but the success of the job is relative to a timeframe. For one, it has to be finite. In our modern world, this is also relative

I don't think there is really any controversy about the existence of those two parameters. Of course, in capitalist society, the ability to provision either abilility or time toward a goal is relative to a whole host of political and capital resources, so there is an arbiter above those barriers that can allocate the resource pools that may be able to accomplish a task. But money and power and ability to make executive decisions is not the third barrier, because it is not something the worker necessarily controls; it's more like happenstance, similar in plane to the construction of the goal itself.

---

The third barrier, maybe obviously, is the will to perform the labor. This is the barrier that I see the least understanding of in my daily life in the Western World, because it is ingrained in identity and is therefore invisible, to an extent. People will not do work they don't believe in, or believe they should have to do. From a functional perspective, this is a barrier to progress, just like the others.

This barrier is harder to discuss because it's a function of incentive.

---

I'm reminded of discussions in the summer of 2025 regarding Pharrell Williams' beat for "So Be It" on Clipse's comeback album *Let God Sort Em Out*. The beat was critically acclaimed for a throwback 00s feel, but I saw a *lot* of discourse in the hip-hop producer communities about how it was overrated. People showed off recreations of it with captions like "did this in 20 minutes". Much was made regarding its unoriginality, and the Western exoticism angle of the sample from Saudi musician Talal Maddah's song "Makah Akloulou".

This conversation irritated me because it clearly identified the reason for the discomfort among those producers in the community: the execution was simple, but you needed the idea. To have the idea, you had to accept some dated notions about American exotica lens and focus on sonic trends of yesteryear. To even have this frame of mind to accept these constraints and work within them, you needed to be freed from the conventions of the modern day. That's a function of will.

There's another example I can recall from 2024 regarding the explosive critical popularity of Magdalena Bay's album *Imaginal Disk* on RateYourMusic, one of the most succintly named websites of all time. As far as I can find, it is the largest aggregator of critical consensus for the medium of music. We have streaming data and sales too, that's important to a degree, but it's also ripe with astroturfing. It's harder to do that on a site like this, even if people can fall into groupthink. *Imaginal Disk* was not a successful project commercially, but it was the far-and-away #1 record of the 2024 on RYM. Of particular controversy was it's relative positioning to Charli XCX's *Brat*, which had one of the most unexpectedly massive cultural impacts in musical history, with the entire summer of 2024 colloquially named after it. It was critically acclaimed and was an absolutely outrageous success. RYM had it #2 for the year and obviously liked it like everyone else, but it was a very distant #2 by rating, and people had strong views about why this hierarchy of the projects had to be crystallized.

What I gleaned from my conversations there was that the opinions centered around a skill/ability argument. Magdalena Bay were capable of creating a vulnerable, abrasive dance-pop record like Brat, but Charli was not capable of creating a sonically dense, narrative project like Imaginal Disk. But is that actually true? Magdalena Bay are protective of their identity as a project that puts out tasteful projects and "good work." Is there any actual argument to be made that they'd be willing to put their name on a project as messy and controversial as Brat? Why would they compromise their assumed identity as a group, or subject themselves to risk - alienation of fans, accusations of "going pop"? You could even say that these indieheads are actually attracted to this unwillingness, associating it to a form of authenticity or dedication to "craft."

I liked Brat more - a lot more, actually. I thought it was weighter and more interesting. It had more history to it, with little callbacks to the annals of UK electronica. It was also just more thrilling in its vulnerability, directly discussing Charli's interpersonal problems with other artists and the dissonance of her values drifting from her artistic persona. This culminated with "I Think About It All the Time", one of the best songs I've ever heard: a confessional about her growing obsession with what motherhood could teach her after a lifetime of rebellion against it. I rarely ever see people talk about this side of the project, because it's all interlaced with buzzsaw dancepop about doing blow and the glamorous inaccessibility of the life behind her fame and fortune.

The power behind pop stars is that they have no problem at all with the barrier of will. They put their name on things that will be perceived en masse and could associate with their names forever. Some of this is risk tolerance, as they swing for upside of commercial success. But some of it is being able to cross lines that other people are too scared to, or to be vulnerable in a hyperperceived setting.

---

Being a capable person with the time to make some sort of headway towards a goal that makes you happy is absolutely worthless unless those resources are accompanied by the will to pursue it.
